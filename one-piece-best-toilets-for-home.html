Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a one-piece and two-piece toilet?
A one-piece toilet fuses the tank and bowl into a single unit with no seam between them, which removes the spot where two-piece toilets most often develop slow leaks over time. Two-piece toilets are usually cheaper and easier to carry during installation since the tank and bowl ship separately, but they rely on a gasket seal between the two pieces that can loosen with age.
Do one-piece toilets need a different rough-in size?
No. Rough-in size, usually 10, 12, or 14 inches measured from the wall to the center of the floor bolts, is independent of whether a toilet is one-piece or two-piece. Most of the one piece best toilets for home use a standard 12-inch rough-in, like the DeerValley model in this guide, so they typically fit existing bathroom plumbing without modification. Measure your existing rough-in before ordering.
Are smart bidet toilets worth the extra cost?
It depends on what would change your daily routine. If a warm-water wash or a heated seat matters to you, the Loupusuo and EPLO picks in this guide deliver those features reliably according to verified owner reviews. If you mainly want a comfortable, leak-resistant toilet at a lower price, a non-smart elongated one-piece model covers that need for several hundred dollars less.
